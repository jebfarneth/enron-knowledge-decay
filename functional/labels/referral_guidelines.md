# Labelling guidelines: referrals

Written on 2026-10-13, after the referral rules were frozen and before any
item of the check sample was drawn or read.

Each item is one email: the date it was sent, its subject, the text its
sender wrote, and one **named person** the detector picked out. You do not
see why it picked them. Answer from the email alone.

**`referral`: does the sender point a reader of the email to the named
person, as someone to go to for something?** `yes`, `no` or `unclear`.

- `yes`: the email tells or suggests that a reader ask, contact, check with,
  talk to, call, email, follow up with, coordinate with, or send or give
  something to the named person; or says the named person will know, can
  help, can answer, or is the person to go to. Questions count too ("Can you
  please call Andre?").
- `no`: the named person is mentioned for another reason (what they did,
  said, know, or are responsible for, without pointing anyone to them), the
  sender says they themselves will contact the person, the name is not a
  person, or the person pointed to is someone else.
- `unclear`: the text does not let you decide.

**`note`**: optional, one line.

**Scoring.** Precision is the share of `yes` among items labelled `yes` or
`no`, with a 95% Wilson interval; `unclear` items are reported separately.
The rules do not change after this check.
